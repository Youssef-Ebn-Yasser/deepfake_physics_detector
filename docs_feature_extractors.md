# Feature Extractors Documentation

This document provides a detailed breakdown of every module inside the `extractors/` folder. These extractors are responsible for calculating the raw physics and biology signatures from the video frames, which are saved as `.npz` files for the Neural Network to consume.

---

## 📷 Sub-System 1: Hardware Optics (Camera Physics)

Deepfakes are generated by software (GANs, Diffusion), meaning they lack the physical imperfections inherently captured by a real camera lens and sensor.

### 1. `lens_distortion.py` ($f_1$)
**What it does:** Measures the geometric lens distortion.
**How it works:** Real lenses are rarely perfect; they bend light slightly, causing radial distortion (barrel/pincushion effects) and tangential distortion. This extractor estimates the camera matrix and distortion coefficients (k1, k2, p1, p2, k3) by tracking feature points across the face.
**Deepfake Flaw:** Deepfake generation often pastes a synthesized face onto a real background without replicating the original lens distortion of the target footage, creating a measurable mathematical mismatch at the boundaries.

### 2. `motion_blur.py` ($f_2$)
**What it does:** Analyzes the relationship between object movement and motion blur.
**How it works:** When a person moves quickly, a real camera shutter stays open long enough to capture motion blur. This extractor computes the **Farneback Dense Optical Flow** to calculate the speed and direction of movement between frames. It then measures the actual blur in the image and compares the two.
**Deepfake Flaw:** Generative AI often struggles with temporal consistency. A deepfake face might move rapidly but remain artificially sharp, violating the physics of shutter speed.

### 3. `frequency_fft.py` ($f_5$)
**What it does:** Analyzes the high-frequency spectrum of the image.
**How it works:** Computes a 2D Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) of the grayscale face and calculates the azimuthal average (a 1D representation of radial frequency distributions).
**Deepfake Flaw:** Deepfakes rely on Up-Sampling layers (like Transposed Convolutions or PixelShuffle) to generate high-resolution faces. These layers leave microscopic "checkerboard" artifacts in the high-frequency spectrum. The FFT easily exposes these unnatural patterns that are invisible to the naked eye.

## 🫀 Sub-System 2: Biological Domain (Human Physiology)

Deepfakes easily fake the *appearance* of a human, but they struggle to fake the *biology* of a human.

### 4. `biomechanics.py` ($f_3$)
**What it does:** Extracts remote Photoplethysmography (rPPG) signals—the human heartbeat.
**How it works:** Every time your heart beats, blood rushes to your face, causing a microscopic change in the skin's color (usually in the green channel). This extractor tracks specific regions of interest (ROI) on the face (forehead, cheeks) over time, applying bandpass filters to isolate the human pulse frequency (usually between 0.75Hz and 2.5Hz).
**Deepfake Flaw:** Because deepfakes generate faces frame-by-frame based on visual texture, they fail to synthesize a consistent, periodic blood-flow color shift. The resulting rPPG signal looks like random noise instead of a heartbeat.

### 5. `lighting_sh.py` ($f_4$)
**What it does:** Estimates the 3D environmental lighting.
**How it works:** Uses MediaPipe to extract a 3D Mesh of the face. By analyzing the normal vectors of the mesh and the pixel colors, it solves for the **Spherical Harmonics (SH)** coefficients. Spherical Harmonics are mathematical functions that describe how light hits a 3D object from all directions.
**Deepfake Flaw:** A common deepfake technique involves generating a face and blending it onto a target body. However, the generated face's lighting often doesn't match the environment the target body is standing in. Comparing the Spherical Harmonics reveals inconsistencies in shadow and light direction.
